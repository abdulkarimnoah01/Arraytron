# Arraytron Stamp changelog

Arraytron Stamp is a Solaris node for placing instances by hand. It is a node of its own (`arraytron::stamp::<version>`), released and updated on its own track, independent of Arraytron's version numbers.

## 0.12.0 - Prototype sockets

* **Import Prototype Inputs** (Brush > Asset, above the Prototype Bank) reads every prototype wired into the Prototype Inputs 2 - 4 (a Reference, an Asset Reference, a merge, a whole node tree: it reads the stage the socket delivers) and adds each one to the Prototype Bank as an Input Connection row with its Prim Path set, so the row stays live. Weight, Scale, Scale Random and Rotation Offset are on every row, and the cards show the prototypes at once.
* **Asset Library:** every prototype also becomes an asset (category Imported): the original USD file when the prototype is a plain reference to one, else a standalone file in `<library>/prototypes`. Each top-level prim of a socket is one prototype; a container of kind group or assembly gives its children; materials, lights, cameras and inactive prims are skipped.
* Click again after wiring more: what is already a row is kept (your weights stay) and only new prototypes are added; Replace The Bank rebuilds the bank from the inputs only; Also Add To The Asset Library switches the library step off. One undo step. `docs/PROTOTYPE_SOCKETS.md`, `tests/validate_protoimport.py`.

## 0.11.0 - Paint Engine 2.0

* **The brush engine is BrushCore 2.0, shared with Arraytron** (`src/brushcore.py`, at the top of the PythonModule): the Paint brush's swept field, the falloff curves and presets are the same code in both tools. `docs/PAINT_ENGINE_2.md`.
* **Paint is a continuous stroke by default** (Brush > Paint > Brush Engine; Classic Dabs is the 0.9 brush, with the 0.9.3 rule: the swept field with a falloff on), with **Strength** and **Density Randomness (Spatter)**; Density and Scale Falloff are on in a new node (preset Sparse Organic); new presets Hard Stamp, Edge Taper, Sparse Organic; new curves Gaussian and Constant (appended to the menu, saved scenes keep their curve).
* **Sculpt brushes** (Brush > Sculpt; a second row of the shelf; C opens a radial menu): **Erase** switches stamps off (Shift: soft thinning; the ids of the others never change; Edit > Restore Erased Stamps / Remove Erased Stamps), **Relax**, **Grow / Shrink** (Sit On Surface keeps the bottom on the ground), **Push / Attract / Drag**, **Replace** (the working set by Weight, placement kept), **Orient / Comb**, **Clone** (Ctrl + click picks the stamps under the brush; click or drag stamps copies, Rotate / Scale / Random Rotate Copies / Conform). Every stroke is one undo step; the stamps move live (held-back writes); Esc during a stroke puts them back.
* **The viewport:** BrushCore's cursor in the brush's colour (teal paint, warm orange erase ...) with the falloff rings; the HUD panel has three levels and hidden (H, a Houdini hotkey; kept in the user's arraytron_brush_prefs.json, shared with Arraytron); the hints follow the brush. A cursor that did not move is not drawn again.
* **Heavy prototypes are fast.** The tool read every mesh of a prototype, render and proxy purposes both (a scanned rock: 1 M render triangles plus its 100 k proxy), built a ray-cast geometry of it and did it again every time the tool resumed (the mouse back in the viewport, after a menu): 3.4 s each time, and 0.7 s for the ghost and 0.6 s for the thumbnail. Now the rays, the ghost and the thumbnails share one copy of each source: the shapes the viewport draws (the proxy instead of the render geometry when there is one, never guides), at most 200 000 triangles (a lighter copy of a denser asset), read once per version of the source (a file by its modification time, a prim by the cook of the inputs) and kept while Houdini runs. The same rock: 0.33 s the first time, 5 ms on every resume; the ghost 0.06 s, then 3 ms. The cook and the render are unchanged (they always used the asset itself).
* **Slot Colors** (Options, on in a new node): every prototype slot of the bank has its own random colour (spread by the golden ratio, so neighbouring slots never look alike), laid over its card (a wash and a band along the bottom) and over the ghost of the next stamp in the viewport (the shading stays), so you see which prototype comes next; the Group brush preview colours each member by its slot, the box drawn for a prototype without geometry takes the colour too. **Color Overlay** sets how strong, **Color Seed** gives another set. The placed stamps keep their own look.
* Tests: `tests/validate_stamplite.py` (402 checks with the heavy group: the proxy, the shared copy, the decimation, the slot colours on the ghost and the cards; mutations purpose and slot; Classic Dabs for its 0.10.0 groups) and `tests/validate_paintengine.py`. 0.10.0 stays next to 0.11.0 (`dist/Arraytron_Stamp_v0.10.0.hda`).

## 0.10.0 - Material variants, a fast Edit mode

* **Material variants, still point instances.** Every bank row has **Materials**: Material prims, or a pattern such as `/Materials/rock_*`. A stamp has a material number: 0 is the prototype's own material, 1 the first of its row's list, 2 the second, and so on. The cook makes one prototype per row and material in use (`p<entry>_<child>_m<k>`, the material bound strongerThanDescendants so it wins over the materials inside the source); material 0 keeps the prototype paths of 0.9. A number past the end of a row's list wraps round; a row without materials is always its own.
* **New stamps** take a material by **Brush > Randomization > Material**: Random (by the Seed, the same Seed gives the same materials; the other random draws are those of 0.9), Fixed Number, or Prototype's Own. Place, Paint (with and without falloff) and Group all use it; a captured Group layout keeps the materials of its stamps.
* **Change them afterwards:** the **Material** column of the STAMPS table (shows `2 rock_red`, edit the number, Set for the selected or all stamps), right click > Material of the selected stamps (Next, Random, Prototype's Own, or a number with its names), **M** in the Edit mode (the next material of each selected stamp), the Edit tab buttons Next Material / Random Material / Prototype's Own Material, and the B options menu. The HUD shows the material of the next stamp.
* **Stamps text:** a line has 12 numbers (`... proto on mat`); lines from before 0.10.0 have 11 and read as material 0, also mixed with new ones in one scene. A captured Group layout line has 9 numbers (8 before).
* **Box select is drawn and selects under the mouse.** The rectangle was drawn around the world origin (the two vectors of Houdini's mapToWorld were read the wrong way round); it now stands on the mouse, just past the near clipping plane so no geometry hides it, and while it grows nothing else is computed.
* **The Edit mode is no longer laggy with many stamps.** The selection boxes are built when the stamps, the selection or the bank change, not on every mouse move, and all at once in numpy (10 000 stamps, 300 or 10 000 selected: a mouse move took 143 ms, now 8 ms); box select uses the four corner rays and numpy instead of projecting every stamp (10 000 stamps: 379 ms, now 39 ms); up to 2000 boxes are drawn (300 before). The Stamps text and the selection are parsed once per change, not on every call.
* **The STAMPS table follows the viewport at once** (it waited for the next refresh of the pane) and sets a selection in one call: 300 selected of 10 000, 119 ms before, 11 ms now; a big old selection is dropped with its selection model instead of being undone range by range; Select All asks for the flags of every cell (110 000 with 10 000 stamps), now a tuple lookup each. The #, On and Row columns are sized from the font (fitting them to the contents read thousands of rows on every update).
* A third demo, **Materials**: random rock materials, trees edited afterwards, a painted stroke of rocks and crates, a Group layout with materials, and 10 000 rocks to try the Edit mode.

## 0.9.4 - Check for Updates tries again

* **Check for Updates tries again when GitHub or the network is busy:** a timeout, a dropped connection or an HTTP 429 / 5xx (502 Bad Gateway, 504) is retried up to four times (a minute to answer each), and when it still fails the message says it is usually temporary and to try again in a minute or two; a 404 says the release may not be published yet.
* A copy that shows "could not reach the update location" has to be updated once by hand (the installer RAR or the zip of this release) to get the retries; after that updates retry by themselves.
* The node is exactly 0.9.3 otherwise.

## 0.9.3 - Paint falloff: density and scale over a swept stroke

* **Dual falloff for the Paint brush.** Brush > Paint > Falloff: **Enable Density Falloff** thins the stamps towards the rim of the brush, **Enable Scale Falloff** shrinks them. Each has a profile (Edge Minimum, Falloff Width, Falloff Strength, Curve: Linear, Smoothstep, Smootherstep, Ease In, Ease Out or a Custom Ramp; the scale one also has a Center Maximum). Presets: Uniform, Soft Natural, Sparse Edges, Growth, Dense Center, Custom. The scale multiplies the stamp's scale (Random Scale included), and Sit On Surface uses the final size.
* **A ribbon, not dabs.** Both channels read one field: the distance to the whole path of the stroke. The candidates are the points of a world-fixed jittered grid with a threshold each, so the stroke has a soft edge with no circles at the dabs, the same stamps at any mouse speed, the same stamps for the same Seed, and painting the same place again adds nothing (the Spacing). Only the new part of the path is processed.
* **Feedback in the viewport:** rings at the inner edge of each fall and the two profiles (density on one side, scale on the other) standing on the surface, and a HUD line.
* Off by default: with both off the brush is the classic one (the Edge Falloff is now called *classic* and is ignored while a new falloff is on). Existing stamps are never changed. A second demo, **Paint Falloff**, with six strokes (uniform, density only, scale only, soft natural, a long S stroke, uneven terrain). Details and measurements: docs/PAINT_FALLOFF_TECH.md.

## 0.9.2 - Group brush: same size by default, layouts, its own randomization

* **Groups are all the same size by default:** the Random Scale of the Randomization tab (for single stamps) no longer scales groups. Brush > Group has its own **Random Group Scale Min / Max** (1 / 1 = off), **Random Member Scale Min / Max** (the members of one group differ, off by default) and **Random Member Rotation** (off by default).
* **Match Member Sizes** (on): every member of a group is scaled to the same size (the median of the selected prototypes), so a small prototype is no longer lost next to a big one; off keeps every prototype's own size.
* **Layouts** (Brush > Group > Layout): Radial, Square, Hexagon, Random Cluster (another one for every group), Line, Spiral, or Custom (the layout of stamps you placed; Set Layout From Selected Stamps switches to it, choosing another layout does not forget it). Count places more members than there are selected cards; Spacing keeps them apart in every layout.
* **Up follows the surface:** Align To Surface is on by default (it was 0) and the up is a **smooth normal** (Placement > Smooth Normals): between the vertices of the mesh you place on, and on the stamps too, creases over 60 degrees stay sharp, so the top of a cube stays flat.
* **Sit On Surface** (Placement, on by default): the bottom of the prototype touches the surface instead of its pivot, so a prototype with its origin in the middle (a character, a toy) stands on the ground or on top of another stamp and does not sink into it. Preview, click, Paint and Group all use it.
* **Faster placing:** the stamps surface reads only the new stamps after a click, a group or a paint dab (3000 stamps: 11 ms before, 4 ms now; 20000: 71 ms before, under 30 ms), the preview no longer parses the whole Stamps text on every mouse move (20000 stamps: 2.9 ms before, 1.3 ms now), and a group's rays only test the stamps near it (a group of 64 on 3000 stamps: 21 ms before, 12 ms now; a group of 7: 2.4 ms before, 1.3 ms).
* The demo has a third node, **Stamp_C_Groups**: the six layouts side by side and a row of groups with the group's own randomization.

## 0.9.1 - managing stamps, a Randomization tab, the stamp cycle you can see, no squashed text

* **Manage the stamps:** under the STAMPS table the buttons Delete, Duplicate, All, None, Invert and Clear All (asks first); Del in the table; a right click on the table (also: select or delete the stamps of the same prototype). A right click on a prototype card selects or deletes the stamps of that prototype (the card tells how many use it) or changes the stamp cycle.
* **Brush > Randomization** (it was Transform): three boxes for what changes between one stamp and the next, Prototype (Random Prototype), Scale (Uniform Scale, Random Scale Min / Max), Rotation (Rotate, Random Rotate, Random Tilt), and the Seed. Align To Surface and Offset moved to the Placement tab (it was Advanced) with the snaps and the drag setting. The help of each says what it does.
* **Which prototypes are in the stamp cycle:** the cards you chose have an orange frame and a number (the order the mousewheel cycles), a line under the shelf names them ("Stamp cycle, 2 selected: 1 Rock, 3 Stone", or "none selected, all 5 prototypes are in play"), and says when Random draws from them.
* The squashed text is fixed: the shelf, the view buttons, the filters and the STAMPS / ASSET LIBRARY headers are as wide as their text (Houdini's style made them as narrow as an icon).

## 0.9.0 - Group brush, Random button, a sheet that edits many stamps

* New **Group brush** (shelf, C menu, Brush > Group): one click stamps several prototypes at once. By default a ring of the selected cards (Ctrl + click adds cards), spaced by their footprints (Spacing); Count draws that many members from the selected cards by Weight, Shuffle Order mixes them for every group. For a custom layout place stamps by hand, select them in the Edit mode and press **G** (or Brush > Group > Set Layout From Selected Stamps): the brush then stamps copies of that cluster, with the rotations and scales you gave them. Drag turns and scales the whole group, Conform To Surface drops every member onto the ground under it, the preview shows every member as the prototype itself, the Random Rotate / Scale / Tilt apply to the group. Reset To Radial Layout goes back.
* The shelf has a **Random** button (Random Prototype): every stamp takes a random one of the selected cards by Weight, and the preview already shows the one that comes next.
* The **STAMPS sheet edits many stamps at once**: a cell you edit changes every selected stamp (select rows in the table, boxes in the viewport, Ctrl + A for all) or all stamps (Edit: All stamps); Set, Add or Multiply (Add for positions and rotations, Multiply for the scale; the prototype Row and On are always set). One undo step, one parameter write. The selection stays while you edit, and every column fits the pane (the Scale column was cut off by a scroll bar).

## 0.8.0 - first public release: the prototype as a ghost, a Paint brush

* The Place brush shows the prototype itself under the mouse (its real geometry, lit and translucent, with the stamp's rotation and scale and the bank row's own Scale, Scale Random and Rotation Offset) instead of a bounding box; the box is only the fallback when the bank cannot be resolved. A dense asset is shown as a lighter copy of itself (about 6000 triangles, points merged on a grid, so the silhouette stays whole). Hovering costs 1 to 2 ms.
* New Paint brush (shelf, C menu, Brush > Paint with Radius, Density, Spacing and Edge Falloff): drag to paint stamps inside a disc that follows the surface, Ctrl + drag to erase, Shift / Ctrl + mousewheel (and [ ]) for the radius and the density. The stamps take the Brush > Transform randomness and the working set; one undo step per stroke.
* Prototype cards with real thumbnails (one per timer tick, no renderer needed), List / Grid / Large views, a search box, favourite stars and filters for the favourites and the working set. Instanceable and animated assets (Houdini's own samples) are measured and drawn.
* Node icon, a Help page, a "Parameters Pane" status line and a Reload Stamp Pane button.
* Layout-grade interaction: Brush and Edit modes (Shift + B), Place and Delete brushes, Houdini hotkeys (C brush menu, B options menu, Z duplicate, Del, Enter, [ ] , .), a working set of prototypes cycled by the mousewheel, Shift / Ctrl drags to scale / orient, the edit handle with box select and duplicate, a HUD panel with a page per brush.
* The Parameters pane: Brush / Edit / Options tabs (Transform, Asset, Advanced, Paint inside Brush), a brush shelf, the STAMPS table and the Asset Library shared with Arraytron (Add to Bank).
* The Prototype Bank of Arraytron (Prim Path, Input Connection, USD File, Group Children; Weight, Scale, Scale Random, Rotation Offset), four inputs, stamps that land on stamps, Place On with several prims and basic shapes.
* Light on purpose: the stamps are one text parameter and the node is one Python LOP (a placement costs about 1 ms, 300 stamps cook in about 5 ms); the mouse ray goes against a hou.Geometry. Houdini 22 and Houdini 20.5 / 21 builds. Not verified by hand in the interface: the feel of the brushes and the edit handle (see docs/ARRAYTRON_STAMP.md).
